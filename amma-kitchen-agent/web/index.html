Two parties, two sets of limits

The customer's spending limits and the merchant's selling rules are separate things, owned by separate people, enforced on separate sides. An order can be stopped by the buyer's own agent before Amma ever hears about it, cleared by the customer and still refused by Amma, or need a human on both sides.

Running the two-person demo

  1. Open /buyer on one screen and /merchant on another — two people, two roles.
  2. As the buyer, order 3 chicken biryanis (₹660). Your own agent refuses it: it is over the ₹600 your customer authorised. Amma is never contacted.
  3. Drop to 2 biryanis (₹440). Now your agent asks you first, because it is over your ₹300 check-with-me line. Approve it as the customer.
  4. Only now does it reach Amma — and it crosses her ₹400 threshold, so it lands in the merchant queue. Approve, refuse, or counter with one biryani instead of two.
  5. The buyer's screen unblocks on its own. Pay with test card 4100 2800 0000 1007, any future expiry and CVV, any 4–10 digit OTP.
  6. Now order the party catering tray. It is affordable and in stock, and it is still refused — and not even Amma can wave that one through.
